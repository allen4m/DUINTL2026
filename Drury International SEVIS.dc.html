SEVIS explained

SEVIS is the system Drury and the Department of Homeland Security use to exchange information about your immigration status. Understanding what it records, and what counts as falling out of status, makes the rules much easier to follow.

What SEVIS is

SEVIS is an internet-based system that allows schools and the United States Department of Homeland Security to exchange data on the immigration status of international students. Accurate and current information is transmitted electronically throughout an F-1 or J-1 student’s academic career in the United States. U.S. embassies and consulates also have access to SEVIS.

How it works

  1. After Drury admits an international student, SEVIS is notified and DHS approves Drury’s request to issue an immigration form (I-20 or DS-2019). Drury sends the new bar-coded form to the student.
  2. The student pays a SEVIS fee and a visa application fee, then visits the U.S. consulate abroad. The consulate confirms through SEVIS that the form is valid and, if everything is in order, issues the visa.
  3. A DHS officer at the U.S. port of entry reports the student’s entry into SEVIS.
  4. On arrival, the student reports to the International Support Services office and Drury confirms enrolment in SEVIS. Drury continues reporting to DHS throughout the student’s academic career.
  5. SEVIS records the student’s departure from the United States.

What it records

  • ›
    Whether you enrolled at the school, or failed to enrol.
  • ›
    A change to your legal name or address, or a dependent’s.
  • ›
    Graduation before the end date listed on the I-20.
  • ›
    Academic or disciplinary action taken due to criminal conviction.
  • ›
    Dropping below a full course of study without prior authorization from the DSO.
  • ›
    Termination date and reason for termination.
  • ›
    Program extensions, school transfers, changes in level of study, employment authorizations, and reinstatement.
  • ›
    Any failure to maintain status or complete your program.

Immigration regulations refer to the international student adviser as the designated school official, or DSO.

Failing to maintain status

Examples include dropping from full-time to part-time enrolment without prior approval from the DSO, attending a school other than the one you are authorized to attend, failing to apply in time for a school transfer, program extension, or change in level of studies, unauthorized employment, and failing to report a change of address within 10 days.

CONSEQUENCES

Students who fail to maintain status lose the privileges of their student immigration status and become subject to deportation. Specific consequences may include denial of re-entry to the U.S., inability to move from undergraduate to graduate status, denial of requests for practical training, denial of requests for change of immigration status, and possible denial of all future visa applications.

If you drop below a full course of study without prior approval, the event is reported to DHS through SEVIS and you are out of status. You may apply to DHS for reinstatement if the violation resulted from circumstances beyond your control. Reinstatement is intended to be a rare benefit for exceptional cases, and you may not apply if you have been out of status longer than five months unless you can show exceptional circumstances such as serious injury or illness, closure of the institution, or a natural disaster. If DHS does not reinstate you, the decision cannot be appealed.

Reduced course load

International students cannot drop below a full course of study without prior authorization from ISS. Acceptable reasons are limited:

  • ›
    Medical problem — 12 months maximum. Documentation from a licensed medical doctor, doctor of osteopathy, or licensed clinical psychologist stating the problem and how long a reduced load is advised. If for two semesters, it must be revalidated each semester.
  • ›
    Initial difficulty with English or reading requirements — first semester only, with a letter from your advisor or instructor.
  • ›
    Unfamiliarity with American teaching methods or improper course placement — one semester only, with a letter from your advisor.
  • ›
    Final semester of your degree.
  • ›
    Concurrent enrolment at another institution, with a transcript supplied.
MINIMUM WITH AUTHORIZATION

Even with an authorized reduced course load you must complete at least 6 credit hours, except for medical reasons or your last semester. Only the designated school official can authorize a reduced credit load.

How to prepare

  • ›
    Read all mail and email from ISS and check the ISS website periodically. Immigration procedures can change quickly.
  • ›
    Learn the regulations and refer questions to ISS rather than to other students. The four words ISS hates to hear are “But my friend said!”
  • ›
    Be proactive. Plan your schedule to maintain full-time enrolment, make travel arrangements early, and anticipate delays at consulates and border crossings. Changes in degree level, program extensions, and travel validations all take processing time.
  • ›
    Come to ISS for help. Drury is a better place because you are here.
