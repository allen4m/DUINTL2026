I-20 and visa

With your enrollment deposit paid and your housing application in, your place at Drury is secured and the visa process begins. Drury issues your Form I-20 for F-1 status, or Form DS-2019 for J-1 status; pay the I-901 SEVIS fee as soon as it arrives, then apply for your visa at a U.S. embassy or consulate. Start early. The process is time sensitive, takes longer than most students expect, and now includes a social media vetting step conducted by the consulate.

A VISITOR VISA WILL NOT WORK

You cannot study at Drury on a Visitor (B) visa or through the Visa Waiver Program. Degree or credit-bearing study requires an F-1 visa, or J-1 for exchange visitors.

PROCESSING TIME

I-20 documents are typically issued within 48 hours after your enrollment deposit and housing application are received.

Receiving your I-20

Once your admission deposit and housing application are complete, Drury issues your Form I-20 and emails it to you as a PDF.

VERIFY YOUR INFORMATION

Check your name, date of birth, program of study, and program start date. Email iadmissions@drury.edu immediately if anything is wrong.

PRINT MULTIPLE COPIES

Print at least two or three. You need the I-20 for your visa interview, for travel, and for immigration check-in at Drury.

SIGN IT

Sign and date the STUDENT ATTESTATION section on page 1. The signature must match your passport.

Transferring from another U.S. school

If you are already studying in the United States, Drury does not issue you a new SEVIS record. Your existing record transfers, so you do not pay the I-901 SEVIS fee again. The process begins once your enrollment deposit and housing application are complete.

  1. 1
    Tell your current school's international office that you intend to transfer, and agree a SEVIS release date with them.
  2. 2
    Your current school releases your SEVIS record to Drury on that date.
  3. 3
    Drury issues a transfer-pending Form I-20 once the record arrives.
  4. 4
    Complete immigration check-in at Drury during International Student Orientation. Your transfer is not finished until you do.

SEVIS I-901 fee

Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee online before scheduling your visa interview. It is required of every F-1 and J-1 student receiving a new SEVIS record. If you are transferring an existing record from another U.S. school, you do not pay it again.

  1. 1
    Go to fmjfee.com(opens in a new tab), the official SEVIS fee payment site.
  2. 2
    Select "PAY I-901 Fee" to start.
  3. 3
    Enter your SEVIS ID, in the top left corner of your I-20.
  4. 4
    Complete the payment form with your personal information.
  5. 5
    Pay the fee: $350 for F-1 students, $220 for J-1 students.
  6. 6
    Print and save your payment confirmation receipt.
BRING THE RECEIPT

Federal law requires proof of payment before you are granted a visa or admitted to the U.S. The consulate can usually verify your payment electronically, but SEVP recommends carrying the receipt: it proves the payment posted correctly and speeds verification, at the interview and again at the port of entry. Reprint it any time at fmjfee.com(opens in a new tab).

Visa interview

Schedule your interview at the U.S. embassy or consulate in your country of residence. The order of steps and the requirements vary by location, so check that post's website. Apply early — interview wait times(opens in a new tab) vary by location and season.

How to apply

  1. 1
    Complete the Form DS-160(opens in a new tab) online nonimmigrant visa application and print the confirmation page.
  2. 2
    Upload your photo during the DS-160. It must meet the State Department photograph requirements.
  3. 3
    Pay the non-refundable visa application fee of $185 before your interview, if your post requires it, plus the $250 visa integrity fee. With the SEVIS fee, an F-1 visa costs $785 in total and a J-1 visa $655. Current amounts are on the State Department fee page(opens in a new tab).
  4. 4
    Schedule your interview at the embassy or consulate.
  5. 5
    Gather the documents listed below.
NEW STUDENTS

F-1 visas can be issued up to 365 days before your program start date. You may not enter the U.S. more than 30 days before the start date on your I-20.

CONTINUING STUDENTS

Visas may be issued at any time, and you may enter at any time before classes start.

The consular officer may also ask for evidence of your academic preparation, your intent to depart the United States when you finish, and how you will pay your educational, living, and travel costs.

IF YOUR VISA IS DELAYED

Contact International Admission immediately. We can often defer your start to the next term and reissue your I-20 rather than have you lose the admission and scholarship you have already earned.

Set up your Drury account

OneLogin is your single sign-on for Drury applications, and it requires multi-factor authentication. Set it up before you leave home: changing phone numbers or SIM cards after arrival makes it harder.

DESKTOP REQUIRED

First-time setup must be completed on a desktop computer.

STEP 1 · SIGN IN

Begin at drury.onelogin.com(opens in a new tab) and enter your Drury email or username — the username is the part of the email before @drury.edu. Review the agreement and click Accept.

STEP 2 · CHOOSE YOUR AUTHENTICATION FACTOR

Setting an authentication factor is required. You may select more than one and change these options later.

  • ›
    OneLogin Protect app — very secure, recommended
  • ›
    Microsoft Authenticator — very secure
  • ›
    Google Authenticator — very secure
  • ›
    Alternate email — less secure. Use a non-Drury address; it receives your login verification token and is also used for password resets.
STEP 3 · SET UP ONELOGIN PROTECT
  1. Download the OneLogin Protect app from your phone's app store.
  2. Open the app and select the + to add a new account.
  3. Scan the QR code with your phone.
  4. Once the app is installed, click Activate on the desktop screen, then select Accept in the OneLogin Protect app.
STEP 4 · SET YOUR PASSWORD

Set your OneLogin/Drury password. It must be at least 14 characters and include one capital letter, one lowercase letter, one number, and one special character.

After your initial login, OneLogin may ask you to download the OneLogin browser extension, which controls password management. This is optional. You then have access to all applications.

FOR INTERNATIONAL STUDENTS

U.S. federal aid, including the FAFSA and federal loans, is generally not available to international students. Your Drury scholarship and any external funding are what you accept in MyDrury.

Your class schedule

Tell Drury as soon as your visa is approved. We then email you a Pre-Scheduling Questionnaire (PSQ), which asks about your academic background and interests and is what we use to build your first class schedule.

Registration order is prioritized by the date your questionnaire is received, so submit it as soon as it arrives. Your admission counselor will make sure you do not miss it.

Required forms

All required forms are completed through MyDrury: sign in with your Drury username and password, click the Student tab, then Student Forms in the left sidebar. If you start a form and run out of time, you can save it and return later.

Travel planning

Once your visa is approved you can book travel. Your F-1 status lets you enter the United States up to 30 days before the program start date on your I-20, and some students use that window to travel in the U.S. first. Arriving in Springfield that early is another matter: residence halls open two days before International Student Orientation, and moving in before then means paying for housing.

Aim to land in the two days before International Student Orientation. That is the window the Student Orientation Team runs complimentary airport pickup at Springfield-Branson National Airport (SGF), and it matches the day residence halls open, so you are met at the airport and can move straight in. Landing earlier means arranging your own transport and paying for hotel.

Check the academic calendar for orientation and semester dates before you book, and book your return flight for after the semester ends.

Once your flights are booked, submit the arrival information form. The Student Orientation Team receives it and arranges your pickup, so this is the only place you need to send your flight details — copying iadmissions@drury.edu is welcome but not required.

CARRY YOUR DOCUMENTS

Keep your passport, visa, I-20, SEVIS fee receipt, financial documents, admission letter, academic transcripts, and health insurance information in your carry-on. Never pack these in checked baggage.

ARRANGE TEMPORARY HOUSING

If you arrive before your move-in date, you need somewhere to stay. Email iadmissions@drury.edu for recommendations.

At the port of entry

A visa does not guarantee entry to the United States. It allows you to travel to a U.S. port of entry and request admission. A U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) officer decides whether to admit you, in what immigration status, and for how long.

  1. 1
    Present your passport, valid visa, and Form I-20 to the CBP officer.
  2. 2
    Answer questions about your studies, your school, and your plans in the United States.
  3. 3
    Provide fingerprints and a photograph, if requested.
  4. 4
    Receive your Form I-94 arrival record after admission, issued electronically for air and sea travel.
  5. 5
    After arrival, retrieve and review your I-94 at i94.cbp.dhs.gov(opens in a new tab). Confirm that CBP recorded your admission as F-1 and that your Admit Until Date is correct. Contact International Support Services (ISS) promptly if any information is incorrect.
    EXPECT A DATE, NOT D/S

    Since 15 September 2026, F-1 students are admitted for a fixed period matching the program length on the I-20, up to four years, so your I-94 may show a specific date rather than D/S.
