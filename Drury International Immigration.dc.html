Fixed period of admission

NEW RULE · SEPTEMBER 15, 2026

On July 17, 2026 the Department of Homeland Security published a final rule replacing duration of status with a fixed period of admission for F and J nonimmigrants. It takes effect on September 15, 2026. Until that date, F students continue to be admitted for duration of status.

The practical change is that your I-94 will carry an Admit Until Date. Staying past it, rather than simply staying enrolled, becomes the thing that puts your status at risk.

WHAT THE RULE SETS

4 years
Maximum admission period, or the program length on your I-20 if shorter
+30 days
Added before your program starts, for arrival
+30 days
Added after, to depart the United States

F-2 dependents also receive a fixed period, which cannot exceed yours, and may need their own extension of stay.

WHEN YOU MUST FILE AN EXTENSION OF STAY

An extension of stay is filed with USCIS, not with Drury. Your DSO still recommends program extensions in SEVIS, but the application itself is yours to submit. You will need one if you:

  • ›Need more time to finish your program
  • ›Are starting a new program of study
  • ›Are moving on to post-completion OPT or the STEM OPT extension

The alternative is to depart the United States and receive a new period of admission from Customs and Border Protection when you re-enter.

IF YOU ARE ALREADY HERE ON DURATION OF STATUS

Staying and not traveling No action is needed before September 15, 2026. You may remain until the program end date on the I-20 valid that day, and in no case beyond November 14, 2030.
Filing for OPT or STEM OPT by March 18, 2027 If you are present in the U.S. on September 15, 2026 and file on time, no extension of stay application is required.
Traveling before you file for OPT If you leave and are readmitted with a fixed period, you must file an extension of stay together with your OPT application. Talk to us before booking.
Cap-gap H-1B beneficiaries No extension of stay is needed; F-1 status continues until the H-1B is approved or April 1 of the relevant fiscal year, whichever comes first.
Finishing a degree after September 15, 2026 You may not enroll in another F-1 program at the same or a lower degree level.
CHECK YOUR DATE, AND CHECK BACK

Everyone should know their own status expiration date under the new framework — after September 15, 2026, staying beyond it can begin to accrue unlawful presence. The rule is subject to congressional review and litigation, and DHS continues to issue guidance, so dates and procedures may change.

Your documents

Four documents matter. Keep the originals safe, carry copies when you travel, and check the expiry dates once a term.

DOCUMENT WHAT IT PROVES WATCH FOR
Passport Your identity and nationality Keep it valid at least six months ahead; renew through your embassy
Visa stamp Permission to enter the United States Needed only to re-enter, not to stay; renewed outside the U.S.
I-20 or DS-2019 Your program, funding, and end date Needs a travel signature before you leave the country
I-94 Your admission record and status Check it after every entry; retrieve it online from CBP

Travel & re-entry

Before you leave the United States for any reason, including a short trip home or to a neighboring country, bring your I-20 or DS-2019 for a travel signature. Plan for this well before your flight rather than the week of.

CARRY WHEN YOU RE-ENTER

Passport, valid visa, signed I-20 or DS-2019, and proof of enrollment. Keep them in your hand luggage, not your checked bag.

IF YOUR VISA HAS EXPIRED

You may stay in the U.S. on a valid I-20 with an expired visa, but you cannot re-enter without renewing it at a consulate abroad. Talk to us before booking travel.

Tell us before you change

Each of these has an immigration consequence, and most are straightforward if handled in advance.

Dropping a course Check with your DSO first if it would take you below full time.
Changing your major Your I-20 must be updated, and it can affect OPT eligibility.
Moving house Report the new address within ten days.
Taking a leave or withdrawing Speak to us before you decide — there are rules about how soon you must depart.
Finishing early or late Both change your program end date and need to be recorded.
Transferring to another school Your SEVIS record is released on a date you and both schools agree. [Drury transfer-out request step — to be supplied]

If something goes wrong

COME TO US FIRST

A lost document, a missed deadline, an accidental drop below full time, a visa denial, or a status violation can often be resolved — reinstatement and correction processes exist. What makes them harder is time. Tell us as soon as you know, even if you think you are at fault.

We cannot give legal advice, but we can explain your options and refer you to an immigration attorney when a case needs one.
